# Policies

## Academic Integrity

All work you submit must be your own, except where group work is explicitly allowed (for example, the Group Assignment). You must not copy solutions from classmates, previous students, solution manuals or online sources, and you must not allow others to copy your work. Cheating in quizzes, tests or the final exam, fabricating data, and helping others to cheat are breaches of academic integrity and will be handled under university regulations.

Discussing ideas with classmates is encouraged. Writing up your solution is an individual task unless told otherwise.

## Referencing

When you use ideas, data, figures or text from another source, you must cite it. Use one of the following styles consistently within a document, and follow any instruction given in the assignment brief:

- **IEEE**: numbered citations, common in engineering.
- **ASME**: used in mechanical engineering publications.
- **APA**: author–date citations.

At minimum, every reference should give the author(s), title, publisher or journal, year and, where relevant, the page or section number.

## Plagiarism and Turnitin

Plagiarism means presenting another person's work or words as your own, including copying without citation, paraphrasing too closely, and reusing your own earlier submissions without permission. Written assignments may be checked with **Turnitin** or similar similarity-detection software. A high similarity score is not by itself proof of plagiarism, but unexplained matches will be investigated, and confirmed plagiarism may lead to loss of marks or further disciplinary action.
